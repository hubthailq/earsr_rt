\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Efficient super-resolution} Since SRCNN~\cite{dong2016srcnn}, SR networks have grown into deep residual and transformer models~\cite{lim2017edsr,ledig2017srgan,liang2021swinir}. A parallel line of work reduces their cost at a fixed quality target, with the NTIRE Efficient SR challenges as the common benchmark~\cite{ren2024ntire,ren2025ntire,ren2026ntire}; examples are RLFN~\cite{kong2022rlfn}, SAFMN~\cite{sun2023safmn}, SMFANet~\cite{zheng2024smfanet} and SPAN~\cite{wan2024span}, the baseline of the recent challenges. Mobile real-time SR has its own challenges~\cite{ignatov2021mobile}. All these models are trained and ranked on DIV2K-style data~\cite{agustsson2017div2k} with bicubic downsampling. We use their published weights unchanged, outside this protocol.

\paragraph{Real-world degradation and compressed inputs} Bicubic-trained models perform worse on real images~\cite{lugmayr2019unsupervised}. Remedies include real paired data~\cite{cai2019realsr}, kernel and noise estimation~\cite{bellkligler2019kernelgan,ji2020realsr}, and synthetic pipelines that randomise blur, noise, resizing and JPEG compression~\cite{zhang2021bsrgan,wang2021realesrgan}, usually combined with adversarial training. Bulat et al.~\cite{bulat2018learn} learn the degradation of real low-resolution faces, and Ohtani et al.~\cite{ohtani2024rethinking} show that compression artefacts in the training data harm SR models. Joint artefact reduction and SR has been studied for natural images~\cite{dong2015arcnn,jiang2021fbcnn,chen2018cisrdcnn}, including a challenge~\cite{yang2022aim} and efficient networks~\cite{ma2024senn}, and general SR methods are sometimes reported below bicubic interpolation on JPEG inputs~\cite{chen2018cisrdcnn}. That compression-aware training helps is therefore established. The cited works do not report the compression level at which efficient SR models stop improving on interpolation, what determines this level, or its effect on real images of a target domain, which are what we measure.

\paragraph{Super-resolution and ear recognition} Resolution and compression are known covariates of ear recognition~\cite{emersic2017uerc,emersic2019uerc,rathgeb2016compression}, and SR has been applied to faces, irises and other traits~\cite{nguyen2018srbiometrics,li2019lowres}. For ears, Luo et al.~\cite{luo2014ear} proposed a recognition-oriented SR method based on label-constrained dictionary learning. Zarachoff et al.~\cite{zarachoff2018ear} compared interpolation, CNN-based and wavelet-based SR on downsampled grayscale ear images with a PCA matcher and found wavelet methods most effective at strong downsampling. Marki\'cevi\'c et al.~\cite{markicevic2023ear} trained EDSR and SwinIR on UERC and reported a rank-1 gain of about one percentage point for EDSR at $\times2$ and a loss for SwinIR, which they attribute to the high quality of the UERC images; this explanation is consistent with our EarVN1.0 probes stored at quality 90 or above, the only group in which a bicubic-trained model improved identification (\RecQHighPub{} points). These studies use uncompressed or undocumented inputs and do not evaluate efficient models; our study adds both, with near-clean ground truth on AMI at three sizes, paired statistics by subject, and identification on real small images of subjects disjoint from all training.
