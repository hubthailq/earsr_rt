\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Efficient super-resolution} Since SRCNN~\cite{dong2016srcnn}, SR networks have developed from a few layers to deep residual and transformer models~\cite{lim2017edsr,ledig2017srgan,liang2021swinir}. A parallel line of work reduces the computational cost at a fixed quality target, with the NTIRE Efficient SR challenges as a common benchmark~\cite{ren2024ntire,ren2025ntire,ren2026ntire}. RLFN~\cite{kong2022rlfn}, SAFMN~\cite{sun2023safmn}, SMFANet~\cite{zheng2024smfanet} and SPAN~\cite{wan2024span} are examples, and SPAN is used as a challenge baseline. Real-time SR on mobile hardware has been the subject of separate challenges~\cite{ignatov2021mobile}. These models are trained and ranked on DIV2K-style data~\cite{agustsson2017div2k} with bicubic downsampling of large natural images. We use their published weights unchanged and examine their behaviour on a domain, an input size and a degradation outside this protocol.

\paragraph{Real-world degradation} It is known that models trained with bicubic downsampling perform worse on real images~\cite{lugmayr2019unsupervised}. This has motivated the collection of real paired data~\cite{cai2019realsr}, the estimation of kernels and noise from real images~\cite{bellkligler2019kernelgan,ji2020realsr}, and synthetic pipelines that randomise blur, noise, resizing and JPEG compression~\cite{zhang2021bsrgan,wang2021realesrgan}. The latter are often combined with adversarial training and aim at perceptual quality on natural images. The quality of the training images has also been studied: Ohtani et al.~\cite{ohtani2024rethinking} report that training data with few compression artefacts lead to better SR models. Bulat et al.~\cite{bulat2018learn} learn the degradation of real low-resolution faces with a generative network and train SR on its outputs, which is related to our use of real images of a biometric trait. Our approach is simpler, since the compression level is read from the image files, and our ablation suggests that for inputs of 24 to 48 pixels the range of compression levels is the main quantity that needs to be matched.

\paragraph{Super-resolution of compressed images} The joint reduction of compression artefacts and SR has been studied for natural images~\cite{dong2015arcnn,jiang2021fbcnn,chen2018cisrdcnn}, has been the subject of a challenge~\cite{yang2022aim}, and has been addressed with efficient networks~\cite{ma2024senn}. Comparisons in this literature include cases in which a general SR method is below bicubic interpolation on JPEG inputs~\cite{chen2018cisrdcnn}. That SR of compressed inputs benefits from compression-aware training is therefore established, and we do not present it as new. In the literature we searched, we did not find a measurement of the compression level at which published efficient SR models stop improving on interpolation for inputs of a few dozen pixels, or a corresponding check on real images of the target domain. Our search was not exhaustive.

\paragraph{Low resolution and compression in ear recognition} Image resolution is one of the covariates analysed in the Unconstrained Ear Recognition Challenges~\cite{emersic2017uerc,emersic2019uerc}, and Rathgeb et al.~\cite{rathgeb2016compression} study the effect of image compression on ear recognition systems. These studies measure recognition performance and do not consider an upscaling step.

\paragraph{Super-resolution for ears and other biometric traits} SR has been applied to face, iris and other biometric modalities~\cite{nguyen2018srbiometrics}, and the benefit of SR for the recognition of real low-resolution faces has been examined~\cite{li2019lowres}. For ears, we found one study, by Marki\'cevi\'c et al.~\cite{markicevic2023ear}, who train EDSR and SwinIR at $\times2$ and $\times4$ on the UERC data and report rank-1 recognition with a ResNet on the super-resolved images, using original and bicubically interpolated images as baselines. Our study differs in its question (which published efficient models are useful on small compressed ear images, and what needs to change when they are not), in its evaluation (clean ground truth at three sizes, three datasets, paired statistics by subject, and identification on real small images with subjects disjoint from all training), and in the real-time constraint.
