\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Efficient super-resolution} Since SRCNN~\cite{dong2016srcnn}, SR networks have grown from a few layers to deep residual and transformer models~\cite{lim2017edsr,ledig2017srgan,liang2021swinir}. A parallel line of work reduces cost under a fixed quality target, with the NTIRE Efficient SR challenges as its reference benchmark~\cite{ren2024ntire,ren2025ntire,ren2026ntire}; RLFN~\cite{kong2022rlfn} and SPAN~\cite{wan2024span} are representative, and SPAN serves as the challenge baseline. All of these models are trained and ranked on DIV2K-style data~\cite{agustsson2017div2k} with bicubic downsampling. We take their published weights as they are and ask how they behave on a domain and an input size the protocol does not cover.

\paragraph{Real-world degradation} That models trained on bicubic downsampling degrade on real images is well known, and has motivated real paired data~\cite{cai2019realsr}, kernel and noise estimation from real images~\cite{bellkligler2019kernelgan,ji2020realsr}, and broad synthetic pipelines that randomise blur, noise, resizing and JPEG compression~\cite{zhang2021bsrgan,wang2021realesrgan}. The latter are usually combined with adversarial training and target perceptual quality on natural images. Our measured degradation is closest in spirit to~\cite{ji2020realsr}; it differs in the regime (inputs of 24 to 48 pixels, where a blur kernel cannot be identified after compression and the compression level, which can be read from the file, dominates), and in that we test the generic alternative directly on the target domain and find it no closer to real small ear images than bicubic downsampling.

\paragraph{Super-resolution of compressed images} Compression artefact removal~\cite{dong2015arcnn,jiang2021fbcnn} and SR of compressed inputs~\cite{yang2022aim} have been studied on natural images at standard sizes. We are not aware of a study that locates the compression level at which published efficient SR models stop being better than interpolation, nor of one at input sizes of a few dozen pixels \todo{confirm after the literature search}.

\paragraph{Super-resolution for ears and biometrics} SR has a long history in face, iris and other biometric modalities~\cite{nguyen2018srbiometrics}. For ears, Markičević et al.~\cite{markicevic2023ear} train EDSR and SwinIR on the UERC data and report rank-1 recognition with the super-resolved images \todo{summarise their degradation model and findings after reading the full text}. We differ in the question asked (which published efficient models work on small compressed ear images and what must change for them to work), in the evaluation (clean ground truth at three sizes, three datasets, paired statistics by subject), and in the constraint of real-time inference.
