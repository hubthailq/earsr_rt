Efficient super-resolution (SR) networks are ranked on large, uncompressed natural images, whereas ear images in the wild are small and JPEG-com\-pressed. We study when they still outperform bicubic interpolation. For \NPub{} published models, four datasets and inputs of \RatioPxMin{} to \RatioPxMax{} pixels, every model reduces the interpolation error but adds an excess to the compression error. This excess grows with compression, whereas the interpolation error saved does not; it lies mainly around the Nyquist frequency of the input and only partly at block boundaries. Ear images are smooth, so the same JPEG quality gives a larger ratio of compression to interpolation error than for natural images, and the gain changes sign at a much lighter compression: at JPEG quality \CrossQAmiMin{} to \CrossQAmiMax{} on AMI against \CrossQNatMin{} to \CrossQNatMax{} on DIV2K, between the two levels most common in real small ear images. Under such compression the architecture hardly changes fidelity, whereas the training degradation does. Fine-tuning a \ParamsSpan{}M-parameter network with JPEG compression over the measured quality range improves on interpolation by \EstVsBicMin{} to \EstVsBicMax{}~dB at \LatPhoneSpanCpu{}~ms on a smartphone CPU, while a \ParamsRatio{}-times larger network trained the same way adds only \RevRrdbVsEstQMin{} to \RevRrdbVsEstQMax{}~dB. On real small EarVN1.0 probes, the fine-tuned network raises rank-1 identification over bicubic upscaling by \RecUpEarvnLgGain{} points, or \RecUpEarvnGain{} when each method has a recogniser trained on its own upscaled images; on AWEx no gain is seen. On synthesised probes of the same subjects, the training that helps identification follows probe compression.
