Ear images captured in unconstrained settings are often small and JPEG-compressed, whereas efficient super-resolution (SR) networks are ranked on large, uncompressed natural images. We evaluate \NPub{} published fidelity-oriented SR networks on ear images with clean ground truth at inputs of 24 to 48 pixels, two in-the-wild ear datasets and natural images. In all \RatioCells{} settings, each model reduces the interpolation error and amplifies the compression error. The models fall below bicubic interpolation once the ratio of the two errors exceeds a dataset-dependent value between \CrossAllMin{} and \CrossAllMax{}, with little dependence on input size. Ear images are smooth and reach this point between JPEG quality 93 and 85, within the range of real small ear images, while natural images reach it between 75 and 60. Under such compression the \NPub{} architectures differ by \SpreadQLow{}~dB, whereas changing the training degradation of one backbone changes PSNR by \DegEffMin{} to \DegEffMax{}~dB. Fine-tuning a \ParamsSpan{}M-parameter network with JPEG compression over the measured quality range improves on bicubic interpolation by \EstVsBicMin{} to \EstVsBicMax{}~dB on three datasets and runs in \LatPhoneSpanCpu{}~ms on the CPU of a 2020 smartphone. On real small images, the rank-1 identification rate increases from \RecRankBic{}\% to \RecRankEst{}\% on EarVN1.0 and does not change on AWEx. AWEx differs in two respects that our data cannot separate: its probes show no JPEG block structure, and the recogniser loses much less accuracy at low resolution.
