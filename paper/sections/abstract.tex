Efficient super-resolution (SR) networks are trained and ranked on uncompressed natural images, and such networks are known to fall below interpolation on JPEG inputs; we locate where this happens for small ear images and what sets the level. For \NPub{} published efficient models, the gain over bicubic interpolation changes sign between JPEG quality \CrossGridAmiLo{} and \CrossGridAmiHi{} on AMI ear images, against \CrossGridNatLo{} and \CrossGridNatHi{} on DIV2K natural images, with the two levels most common in real small ear images on either side. The level depends on image content: in all \RatioCells{} settings tested, and with WebP and AVIF, every model adds an excess to the error that compression causes in interpolation, and smooth ear images have little interpolation error to set against it. This applies to crops that still carry their compression error; compressing before downsizing returns all models above interpolation. A \ParamsSpan{}M-parameter network fine-tuned with JPEG compression over the quality range read from the files improves on interpolation by \EstVsBicMin{} to \EstVsBicMax{}~dB at \LatPhoneSpanCpu{}~ms on a smartphone CPU, whereas a \ParamsRatio{}-times larger network trained the same way adds \RevRrdbVsEstQMin{} to \RevRrdbVsEstQMax{}~dB at quality 75; on clean inputs the fine-tuned network is below its published weights. With a recogniser trained for each method on its own upscaled images, it raises the rank-1 rate of real small EarVN1.0 probes from \RecUpEarvnBic{}\% to \RecUpEarvnEst{}\%; no gain is seen on AWEx.
