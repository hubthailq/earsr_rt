Efficient super-resolution (SR) networks are ranked on large, uncompressed natural images, whereas ear images in the wild are small and JPEG-compressed. We study when such networks still outperform bicubic interpolation. For \NPub{} published models, four datasets and inputs of \RatioPxMin{} to \RatioPxMax{} pixels, every model reduces the interpolation error but adds an excess to the compression error. This excess grows with compression for every model and dataset, whereas the interpolation error saved, measured on clean inputs, does not. Ear images are smooth, so the same JPEG quality gives a much larger ratio of compression error to interpolation error than for natural images, and the gain changes sign at a much lighter compression: at JPEG quality \CrossQAmiMin{} to \CrossQAmiMax{} on AMI against \CrossQNatMin{} to \CrossQNatMax{} on DIV2K, between the two quality levels most common in real small ear images. Under such compression the architecture hardly changes fidelity, whereas the training degradation does. Fine-tuning a \ParamsSpan{}M-parameter real-time network with JPEG compression over the measured quality range improves on interpolation by \EstVsBicMin{} to \EstVsBicMax{}~dB at \LatPhoneSpanCpu{}~ms on a 2020 smartphone CPU, while a \ParamsRatio{}-times larger network trained the same way adds only \RevRrdbVsEstQMin{} to \RevRrdbVsEstQMax{}~dB. On real small probes, rank-1 identification rises from \RecRankBic{}\% to \RecRankEst{}\% on EarVN1.0, but no gain of this size is seen on AWEx, which differs in compression and in the accuracy lost at low resolution. On EarVN1.0, LPIPS follows the identification ranking across methods, whereas PSNR does not once GAN-trained models are included.
