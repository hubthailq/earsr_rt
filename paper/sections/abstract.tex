Ear images captured in unconstrained settings are small and JPEG-compressed, whereas efficient super-resolution (SR) networks are designed and ranked on large, uncompressed natural images. We audit \NPub{} published fidelity-oriented SR networks, including the NTIRE 2026 efficient-SR baseline and five of its entries, on ear images with clean ground truth at inputs of 24 to 48 pixels, on two in-the-wild ear datasets and, as a control, on natural images. Their advantage over bicubic interpolation is governed by one quantity, the error added by compression relative to the interpolation error: across \RatioSets{} datasets, input sizes of \RatioPxMin{} to \RatioPxMax{} pixels and JPEG qualities of 60 to 93, the models are above bicubic interpolation when this ratio is below about \RatioThresh{} and below it otherwise. Input size plays no role. Because ear images are smooth, they cross the threshold between quality 93 and 85, inside the range of real small ear images; natural images cross it between 75 and 60. Beyond the threshold the \NPub{} architectures differ by only \SpreadQLow{}~dB and a \ParamsRatio{}$\times$ larger network is no better, whereas changing the training degradation of one real-time backbone recovers \DegEffMin{} to \DegEffMax{}~dB. Fine-tuning a \ParamsSpan{}M-parameter network with compression over the measured range of levels, under a recipe that prevents a collapse we observe when fine-tuning on laboratory data, exceeds bicubic interpolation by \EstVsBicMin{} to \EstVsBicMax{}~dB and the published weights by \EstVsPubMin{} to \EstVsPubMax{}~dB on three datasets, at \LatSpan{}~ms per image on a desktop GPU. On real small images, for which no ground truth exists, identification shows that upscaling helps only where the probes are compressed and the recogniser loses accuracy at low resolution: the rank-1 rate rises from \RecRankBic{}\% to \RecRankEst{}\% on EarVN1.0 and is unchanged on AWEx.
