Ear images in the wild are often small and JPEG-compressed, whereas efficient super-resolution (SR) networks are ranked on large, uncompressed natural images. We evaluate \NPub{} published fidelity-oriented SR networks on ear images with clean ground truth at 24- to 48-pixel inputs, two in-the-wild ear datasets and natural images. In all \RatioCells{} settings, each model reduces the interpolation error and amplifies the compression error. The models fall below bicubic interpolation once the ratio of these errors exceeds a dataset-dependent value between \CrossAllMin{} and \CrossAllMax{}, with little dependence on input size. For smooth ear images this point lies between JPEG quality 93 and 85, within the range of real small ear images; for natural images it lies between 75 and 60. Under such compression the \NPub{} architectures differ by \SpreadQLow{}~dB, whereas changing the training degradation of one backbone changes PSNR by \DegEffMin{} to \DegEffMax{}~dB. A \ParamsSpan{}M-parameter network fine-tuned with JPEG compression over the measured quality range improves on bicubic interpolation by \EstVsBicMin{} to \EstVsBicMax{}~dB on three datasets and runs in \LatPhoneSpanCpu{}~ms on a 2020 smartphone CPU. On real small images, the rank-1 identification rate increases from \RecRankBic{}\% to \RecRankEst{}\% on EarVN1.0 and is unchanged on AWEx, which differs in compression and in the accuracy lost at low resolution, two factors our data cannot separate. Compression removal before upscaling gives a comparable PSNR with \RevFbParamsRatio{} times the parameters, and a large published model trained with synthetic degradations gives a higher rank-1 rate with one of two recognisers.
