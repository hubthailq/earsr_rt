Efficient super-resolution (SR) networks are trained on uncompressed natural images, yet small ear images in forensic and surveillance use are usually JPEG-compressed. We show where and why such networks stop helping. For \NPub{} published efficient models, the median gain over bicubic interpolation changes sign between JPEG quality \CrossGridAmiLo{} and \CrossGridAmiHi{} on ear images (AMI), but between \CrossGridNatLo{} and \CrossGridNatHi{} on natural images (DIV2K). An error decomposition shows that every model amplifies the compression error of its input ($b>1$ in all \RatioCells{} settings, and with WebP and AVIF). \KeyFinding. The two compression levels most common in real small ear images lie on either side of this point, for crops that keep their compression error. Fine-tuning a \ParamsSpan{}M-parameter network over the JPEG quality range read from the target files improves on interpolation by \EstVsBicMin{} to \EstVsBicMax{}~dB at \LatPhoneSpanCpu{}~ms on a smartphone CPU and, with recognisers trained on upscaled images, raises rank-1 identification of real small EarVN1.0 probes from \RecUpEarvnBic{}\% to \RecUpEarvnEst{}\%, though not on AWEx.
