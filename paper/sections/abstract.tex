Efficient super-resolution (SR) networks are ranked on large, uncompressed natural images, whereas ear images in the wild are small and JPEG-com\-pressed. We study when they still outperform bicubic interpolation. For \NPub{} published models, four datasets and inputs of \RatioPxMin{} to \RatioPxMax{} pixels, every model reduces the interpolation error but adds an excess to the compression error, which grows with compression and lies mainly around the Nyquist frequency of the input. Ear images are smooth, so the same JPEG quality gives a larger compression error relative to the interpolation error, and the gain changes sign at a much lighter compression than for natural images: at quality \CrossQAmiMin{} to \CrossQAmiMax{} on AMI against \CrossQNatMin{} to \CrossQNatMax{} on DIV2K, between the two levels most common in real small ear images. The excess also occurs with WebP and AVIF, and all models exceed interpolation again when the large image is compressed before downsizing. Under such compression the architecture hardly changes fidelity, whereas the training degradation does. Fine-tuning a \ParamsSpan{}M-parameter network with JPEG compression over the measured quality range improves on interpolation by \EstVsBicMin{} to \EstVsBicMax{}~dB at \LatPhoneSpanCpu{}~ms on a smartphone CPU, while a \ParamsRatio{}-times larger network trained the same way adds only \RevRrdbVsEstQMin{} to \RevRrdbVsEstQMax{}~dB. On real small EarVN1.0 probes, the fine-tuned network raises rank-1 identification over bicubic upscaling by \RecUpEarvnLgGain{} points, or \RecUpEarvnGain{} when each method has a recogniser trained on its own upscaled images; on AWEx no gain is seen.
