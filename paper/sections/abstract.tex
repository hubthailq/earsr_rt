Efficient super-resolution (SR) networks are ranked on large, uncompressed natural images, whereas ear images in the wild are small and JPEG-com\-pressed. We study when such networks still outperform bicubic interpolation. We write the error of a model on a compressed input as $a$ times the interpolation error of the clean input plus $b$ times the error that compression adds to interpolation. For \NPub{} published models trained with bicubic downsampling, four datasets and inputs of \RatioPxMin{} to \RatioPxMax{} pixels, $b$ exceeds 1 in every case: each model adds an excess to the compression error, which grows with compression and lies mainly around the Nyquist frequency of the input. The gain over interpolation therefore changes sign at a compression level that depends on image content. Ear images are smooth, and on AMI the sign changes between JPEG quality \CrossGridAmiLo{} and \CrossGridAmiHi{}, against \CrossGridNatLo{} and \CrossGridNatHi{} on DIV2K; the two levels most common in real small ear images lie on either side. The excess also occurs with WebP and AVIF. As an application, fine-tuning a \ParamsSpan{}M-parameter network with JPEG compression over the measured quality range improves on interpolation by \EstVsBicMin{} to \EstVsBicMax{}~dB at \LatPhoneSpanCpu{}~ms on a smartphone CPU, and under compression the training degradation changes fidelity much more than the architecture. On real small EarVN1.0 probes, with a recogniser trained for each method on its own upscaled images, the rank-1 rate rises from \RecUpEarvnBic{}\% to \RecUpEarvnEst{}\%; on AWEx no gain is seen.
