\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The ear can be photographed at a distance and without the cooperation of the subject, which makes it useful in surveillance and forensic settings~\cite{emersic2017ear}. In such images the ear often occupies a few dozen pixels and the file is JPEG-compressed: in EarVN1.0~\cite{hoang2019earvn}, several thousand ear crops have a short side of 24 to 48 pixels, all stored with lossy compression. Both factors reduce recognition accuracy~\cite{emersic2019uerc,rathgeb2016compression}. Enlarging such images, for an operator or for a recogniser, is a natural use of efficient single-image super-resolution (SR), whose networks run in milliseconds~\cite{ren2024ntire,ren2025ntire,ren2026ntire}. These networks, however, are designed and ranked on large natural images with bicubic downsampling and no compression. Previous ear SR studies~\cite{luo2014ear,zarachoff2018ear,markicevic2023ear} use uncompressed or undocumented inputs and do not evaluate efficient models.

SR models trained with bicubic downsampling are known to lose accuracy on compressed inputs~\cite{lugmayr2019unsupervised,chen2018cisrdcnn}, and training with compression is an established remedy~\cite{zhang2021bsrgan,wang2021realesrgan}. These works do not report at which compression level the loss occurs for efficient models, what determines that level, or whether it matters for real ear images. We measure all three with \NPub{} published efficient SR models, three ear datasets and a control on natural images.

The main finding concerns the error that compression adds. In all \RatioCells{} settings we tested, every model reduces the interpolation error but adds an excess to the compression error of interpolation; the excess grows with compression, whereas the interpolation error saved does not. It lies mainly around the Nyquist frequency of the input and only partly at JPEG block boundaries. Ear images are smooth, so the same JPEG quality gives a much larger ratio of compression error to interpolation error than for natural images (\RatioAmiQLow{} on AMI against \RatioDivQLow{} on DIV2K at quality 75), and the gain changes sign at a much lighter compression: at quality \CrossQAmiMin{} to \CrossQAmiMax{} on AMI against \CrossQNatMin{} to \CrossQNatMax{} on DIV2K. The two most common quality levels of real small ear images, 75 (\QLowShare{}\%) and 93 (\QHighShare{}\%), lie on either side of this point.

Two consequences follow. First, under compression the training degradation matters much more than the architecture. At quality 75 the published architectures differ by \SpreadQLow{}~dB, whereas fine-tuning one real-time backbone with JPEG compression over the measured range of quality levels changes its PSNR by \DegEffMin{} to \DegEffMax{}~dB; a network with \ParamsRatio{} times more parameters, fine-tuned in the same way, adds only \RevRrdbVsEstQMin{} to \RevRrdbVsEstQMax{}~dB. Second, the same match between training degradation and input appears in identification. On real EarVN1.0 probes the model trained with compression raises the rank-1 rate from \RecRankBic{}\% to \RecRankEst{}\%, and about half of this gain remains when each method has a recogniser trained on its own upscaled images. On probes synthesised from large images of the same subjects, the method that helps reverses with the compression of the probe: bicubic-trained models help on clean probes, models trained with compression on compressed ones. On AWEx no gain is seen.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item An error decomposition for \NPub{} published efficient SR models: every model adds an excess to the compression error that grows with compression, and the compression level at which the gain over interpolation changes sign depends mainly on image content and on the codec, and less on input size.
\item A benchmark of small ear images with near-clean ground truth on AMI at three input sizes, two in-the-wild datasets that serve as further checks with less clean ground truth, and a natural-image control, with checks of the order of degradations (offset block grid, double compression).
\item Controlled comparisons showing that, under compression, the training degradation changes fidelity far more than the architecture, among published models and under identical training. A real-time SPAN fine-tuned over the JPEG range of the target images improves on bicubic interpolation by \EstVsBicMin{} to \EstVsBicMax{}~dB and on the published weights by \EstVsPubMin{} to \EstVsPubMax{}~dB on three datasets, and runs in \LatPhoneSpanCpu{}~ms on a smartphone CPU.
\item An identification study on real and synthesised small probes, in which the upscaler that helps is the one whose training degradation matches the compression of the probe.
\end{enumerate}

We do not propose a new architecture, and compression-aware training is established; what the analysis adds is when such training is needed and which compression range to cover. On clean inputs the adapted model is below the published weights (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
