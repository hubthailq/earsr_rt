\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
For each backbone and fold we fine-tune one model per training degradation (bicubic, generic, bicubic followed by JPEG 75, and measured), all with the procedure of Section~\ref{sec:stable}. With the ablation runs, this gives \NRuns{} runs over \NFolds{} folds. Models are compared with bicubic interpolation and with the published weights of the same backbone on AMI at 36-pixel inputs (\NAmiImg{} images of \NAmiSubj{} subjects), EarVN1.0 at 24 pixels (\NEarvnImg{} images of \NEarvnSubj{} subjects) and AWEx at 24 and 36 pixels (\NAwexSImg{} and \NAwexMImg{} images).

\subsection{Training degradation and architecture}
Fine-tuning SPAN on ear images with bicubic downsampling improves clean inputs but leaves it below interpolation under compression, by a margin similar to that of the published weights (Table~\ref{tab:matrix}): target-domain data do not compensate for a mismatched degradation. The generic pipeline is below interpolation on clean inputs (by \GenVsBicCleanMin{} to \GenVsBicCleanMax{}~dB) and within \GenVsBicEstMin{} to \GenVsBicEstMax{}~dB of it on measured inputs. On JPEG 75 inputs, training with the measured degradation instead of bicubic downsampling gains \DegEffMin{} to \DegEffMax{}~dB, and training with fixed JPEG 75 gains \JpgEffMin{} to \JpgEffMax{}~dB, against a \SpreadQLow{}~dB range among the published architectures.

To compare architectures under identical training, we also fine-tune the published RRDB weights, with \ParamsRatio{} times the parameters of SPAN, by the same procedure (mixed precision, to fit memory; all runs pass the stress test). At JPEG 75 RRDB gains \RevRrdbGainQMin{} to \RevRrdbGainQMax{}~dB over its published weights, against \RevSpanGainQMin{} to \RevSpanGainQMax{}~dB for SPAN, and exceeds the fine-tuned SPAN by \RevRrdbVsEstQMin{} to \RevRrdbVsEstQMax{}~dB (\RevRrdbVsEstMin{} to \RevRrdbVsEstMax{}~dB over all compressed inputs; Table~\ref{tab:review} in \ref{app:alternatives}). For fidelity under compression, the training degradation thus changes either network by more than a decibel and the architecture by a fraction of a decibel.

\subsection{Comparison with the references}
On measured inputs, SPAN fine-tuned with the measured degradation improves on bicubic interpolation by \EstVsBicMin{} to \EstVsBicMax{}~dB, on the published weights by \EstVsPubMin{} to \EstVsPubMax{}~dB and on the generic pipeline by \EstVsGenMin{} to \EstVsGenMax{}~dB, with confidence intervals excluding zero (Table~\ref{tab:main}). LPIPS also improves. The advantage holds in every fold, including the two held-out folds, at single quality levels (\EstVsBicQLowMin{} to \EstVsBicQLowMax{}~dB over interpolation at quality 75, \EstVsBicQHighMin{} to \EstVsBicQHighMax{}~dB at 93), for DISP (\DispEstVsBicMin{} to \DispEstVsBicMax{}~dB) and with an offset block grid or double compression (\ref{app:order}). It is largest for the smallest inputs (\EstVsBicAmiS{}, \EstVsBicAmiM{} and \EstVsBicAmiL{}~dB at 24, 36 and 48 pixels; Figure~\ref{fig:size}). The cost is on clean inputs, where the model is \EstVsPubCleanMin{} to \EstVsPubCleanMax{}~dB below the published weights while remaining above interpolation.

Published models of two other kinds also account for compression (\ref{app:alternatives}). FBCNN~\cite{jiang2021fbcnn} followed by the published SPAN, which removes compression artefacts before upscaling, is within \RevFbVsEstAmiMin{} to \RevFbVsEstAmiMax{}~dB of our model on compressed AMI inputs and keeps more accuracy on clean inputs, with \RevFbParamsRatio{} times the parameters. BSRNet~\cite{zhang2021bsrgan} and Real-ESRNet~\cite{wang2021realesrgan}, RRDB networks trained with synthetic degradations, stay within \RevBsrVsBicMin{} to \RevBsrVsBicMax{}~dB of interpolation or fall below it.

\subsection{Components of the measured degradation}
\label{sec:ablation}
Relative to fixed JPEG 75, the measured degradation is better on quality-93 inputs (\EstVsJpgHighMin{} to \EstVsJpgHighMax{}~dB) and slightly worse at quality 75 (\EstVsJpgLowMin{} to \EstVsJpgLowMax{}~dB; \ref{app:levels}). Two further models, one with the measured JPEG levels only and one with JPEG quality drawn uniformly from 60 to 95, are each best on the inputs that match their training and differ from each other by \MixVsUniMin{} to \MixVsUniMax{}~dB. JPEG compression over the range found in the target images, about 70 to 95 here, is therefore the component that matters; we found no evidence that estimating noise and blur is needed.

\paragraph{Training variation} The confidence intervals are computed over subjects. Retraining three models of fold 1 with two further seeds changes PSNR by at most \SeedMaxRange{}~dB (median \SeedMedRange{}~dB) over \SeedCells{} combinations, and \SeedFlips{} of the \SeedDiffs{} differences between models changes sign. We do not rank models on differences of a few hundredths of a decibel.

\subsection{Identification on real small ear images}
\label{sec:recog}
Real small images have no ground truth, but they have identities. We enrol \RecEnrolled{} EarVN1.0 subjects not used for any training or fitting with half of their large images (\RecGallery{} images, averaged into templates) and use as probes their \RecProbes{} real small images (short side 24 to 48 pixels, \RecSubj{} subjects), enlarged $\times4$ by each method. A recogniser trained only on large images has never seen upscaled images, and the gain of an upscaler may then reflect how closely its output resembles large images rather than recovered identity. Our main protocol therefore trains, for each of bicubic upscaling, the published SPAN weights and our model, a separate ResNet-18~\cite{he2016resnet} with a cosine-margin loss~\cite{wang2018cosface} on large images of \RecTrainSubj{} other subjects together with their \RecUpNSmall{} real small images upscaled by that method, and evaluates each method with its own recogniser.

With this protocol, our model raises the rank-1 rate on EarVN1.0 (Table~\ref{tab:recogup}) from \RecUpEarvnBic{}\% to \RecUpEarvnEst{}\%, by \RecUpEarvnGain{} points (95\% interval \RecUpEarvnGainLo{} to \RecUpEarvnGainHi{}), and the published SPAN weights remain close to bicubic upscaling (\RecUpEarvnPub{}\%). Absolute rates remain low; large probes reach \RecUpEarvnRef{}\%. On AWEx (\RecAwexEnrolled{} subjects enrolled, \RecAwexProbes{} probes, never used for training) the three methods are indistinguishable (\RecUpAwexBic{}\%, \RecUpAwexPub{}\% and \RecUpAwexEst{}\%).

\paragraph{Recognisers trained on large images} To compare all methods with one recogniser, we also use Ear-R18, trained with the same recipe on large images only (\RecValAcc{}\% accuracy on held-out images), Ear-R50, a ResNet-50 trained on the same data, and frozen ImageNet features as a control (\ref{app:recoglarge}). With Ear-R18 the gain of our model is larger, from \RecRankBic{}\% to \RecRankEst{}\% (\RecUpEarvnLgGain{} points), so about half of it reflects the mismatch between upscaled probes and the training images of the recogniser. Across the methods, bicubic-trained models barely change the rate (\RecPubVsBic{} points for the published SPAN weights), and every method that accounts for compression raises it, including FBCNN followed by SPAN, BSRNet and Real-ESRNet. Identification does not follow PSNR: BSRNet, with a PSNR close to interpolation, reaches \RevRankBsr{}\% and \RevRankBsrRf{}\% with the two ear recognisers, the highest rate with Ear-R50, whereas FBCNN followed by SPAN has a higher PSNR and a lower rate (\RevRankFb{}\% and \RevRankFbRf{}\%). As for fidelity, the published SPAN weights help only for probes stored at quality 90 or above (\RecQHighPub{} points, against \RecQLowPub{} at quality 70 to 80), and on probes synthesised from large images the ordering of bicubic-trained and compression-trained models depends on their compression (\ref{app:recogsynth}). On AWEx no method changes the rate significantly with any recogniser; the intervals, about 8 points wide, exclude only gains of the EarVN1.0 size. The results do not show that SR improves ear identification in general.

\subsection{Efficiency}
\label{sec:efficiency}
The proposed training leaves the cost of the backbone unchanged. We define real time as a median latency of at most 33~ms for one $48\times68$ input, the largest size considered. On an RTX~3080, SPAN takes \LatSpan{}~ms and DISP \LatDisp{}~ms, against \LatBsrgan{}~ms for BSRGAN. On an \PhoneName{} (\PhoneChip{}, iOS \PhoneOs{}, released in 2020), with Core ML in 16-bit precision, SPAN takes \LatPhoneSpanCpu{}~ms on the CPU and \LatPhoneSpanAll{}~ms with all compute units (Table~\ref{tab:latencyios}); converted and PyTorch outputs agree up to 16-bit rounding (\CoremlPsnrSpan{}~dB). The RRDB architecture of BSRGAN, BSRNet and Real-ESRNet takes \LatPhoneRrdbCpu{}~ms on the CPU and \LatPhoneRrdbAll{}~ms with the neural engine, so it meets the threshold only with the accelerator, where the small network's advantage becomes the time and memory left for other steps. Latency and identification are plotted together in \ref{app:alternatives}.
