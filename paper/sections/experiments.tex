\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
For each backbone and fold we fine-tune one model per training degradation (bicubic, generic, bicubic followed by JPEG 75, and measured), all with the procedure of Section~\ref{sec:stable}. With the ablation runs, this gives \NRuns{} runs over \NFolds{} folds. Models are compared with bicubic interpolation and with the published weights of the same backbone on AMI at 36-pixel inputs (\NAmiImg{} images of \NAmiSubj{} subjects), EarVN1.0 at 24 pixels (\NEarvnImg{} images of \NEarvnSubj{} subjects) and AWEx at 24 and 36 pixels (\NAwexSImg{} and \NAwexMImg{} images).

\subsection{For fidelity, the training degradation matters more than the architecture}
Fine-tuning SPAN on ear images with bicubic downsampling improves clean inputs but leaves it below interpolation under compression, by a margin similar to that of the published weights (Table~\ref{tab:matrix}): target-domain data do not compensate for a mismatched degradation. The generic pipeline is below interpolation on clean inputs (by \GenVsBicCleanMin{} to \GenVsBicCleanMax{}~dB) and within \GenVsBicEstMin{} to \GenVsBicEstMax{}~dB of it on measured inputs; it wins only on its own inputs, which the classifier separates from real images. On JPEG 75 inputs, training with the measured degradation instead of bicubic downsampling gains \DegEffMin{} to \DegEffMax{}~dB, and training with fixed JPEG 75 gains \JpgEffMin{} to \JpgEffMax{}~dB, more than ten times the \SpreadQLow{}~dB range of the published architectures.

\subsection{Comparison with the references}
On measured inputs, SPAN fine-tuned with the measured degradation improves on bicubic interpolation by \EstVsBicMin{} to \EstVsBicMax{}~dB, on the published weights by \EstVsPubMin{} to \EstVsPubMax{}~dB and on the generic pipeline by \EstVsGenMin{} to \EstVsGenMax{}~dB, with confidence intervals excluding zero (Table~\ref{tab:main}). LPIPS also improves, by \EstLpipsVsBicMin{} to \EstLpipsVsBicMax{} over interpolation and \EstLpipsVsGenMin{} to \EstLpipsVsGenMax{} over the generic pipeline. The advantage holds in every fold, including the two held-out folds (\EstVsGenFoldMin{} to \EstVsGenFoldMax{}~dB over the generic pipeline on AMI), at single quality levels (\EstVsBicQLowMin{} to \EstVsBicQLowMax{}~dB over interpolation at quality 75, \EstVsBicQHighMin{} to \EstVsBicQHighMax{}~dB at 93), and for DISP (\DispEstVsBicMin{} to \DispEstVsBicMax{}~dB over interpolation). It is largest for the smallest inputs (\EstVsBicAmiS{}, \EstVsBicAmiM{} and \EstVsBicAmiL{}~dB at 24, 36 and 48 pixels; Figure~\ref{fig:size}). The cost is on clean inputs, where the model is \EstVsPubCleanMin{} to \EstVsPubCleanMax{}~dB below the published weights while remaining above interpolation; since the real small ear images we examined are all compressed, we accept this trade-off.

\subsection{Components of the measured degradation}
\label{sec:ablation}
Relative to fixed JPEG 75, the measured degradation is better on quality-93 inputs (\EstVsJpgHighMin{} to \EstVsJpgHighMax{}~dB) and on the measured mixture (\EstVsJpgMin{} to \EstVsJpgMax{}~dB) and slightly worse at quality 75 (\EstVsJpgLowMin{} to \EstVsJpgLowMax{}~dB; Table~\ref{tab:levels} in \ref{app:levels}). Covering the range of levels that occurs in the data is therefore what matters. Two further models isolate the noise and blur (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}): one with the measured JPEG levels only, one with JPEG quality drawn uniformly from 60 to 95. Each fold is trained with one seed, and the confidence intervals, computed over subjects, do not include training variation; we therefore do not rank models that differ by a few hundredths of a decibel, even where the interval excludes zero. The differences are of this size: each model is best on the inputs that match its training (the full measured model by \EstVsMixMin{} to \EstVsMixMax{}~dB on measured inputs, the levels-only model by \MixVsEstOnMixMin{} to \MixVsEstOnMixMax{}~dB on JPEG-only inputs), the two JPEG-only models differ by \MixVsUniMin{} to \MixVsUniMax{}~dB, and identification on real images (Section~\ref{sec:recog}) does not separate them. The component that matters is therefore JPEG compression over the range found in the target images, about 70 to 95 here. We found no evidence that estimating noise and blur is needed, and keep the measured degradation as reference because it was specified before these comparisons.

\subsection{Identification on real small ear images}
\label{sec:recog}
Real small images have no ground truth, but they have identities. We enrol \RecEnrolled{} EarVN1.0 subjects not used for any training or fitting with half of their large images (\RecGallery{} images, averaged into templates) and use as probes their \RecProbes{} real small images (short side 24 to 48 pixels, \RecSubj{} subjects), enlarged $\times4$ by each method. The recogniser is a ResNet-18~\cite{he2016resnet} trained with a cosine-margin loss~\cite{wang2018cosface} on large images of \RecTrainSubj{} other subjects (\RecValAcc{}\% accuracy on held-out images of these subjects), never on small or upscaled images; frozen ImageNet features serve as a control. With large probes it reaches a rank-1 rate of \RecRankRef{}\%; with small probes and bicubic upscaling, \RecRankBic{}\% (Table~\ref{tab:recog}).

Bicubic-trained models barely change this rate: the published SPAN weights reach \RecRankPub{}\% (\RecPubVsBic{} points) and SPAN fine-tuned with bicubic downsampling \RecRankBict{}\%. Models trained with compression do: SPAN with the measured degradation reaches \RecRankEst{}\% (\RecEstVsBic{} points over bicubic upscaling, \RecEstVsPub{} over the published weights; EER from \RecEerBic{}\% to \RecEerEst{}\%), and DISP \RecRankDispEst{}\%. The compression-only variants are within about one point of each other, and the generic pipeline is slightly lower (\RecGenVsEst{} points relative to our model, and \RecEarvnRfGenVsEst{} with the ResNet-50 recogniser introduced below). BSRGAN and Real-ESRGAN \RecGanPair{}, not significantly different from our model (\RecBsrganVsEst{} points for BSRGAN) at \LatRatio{} times the latency. The control recogniser gives the same ordering (\RecCtlRankBic{}, \RecCtlRankPub{} and \RecCtlRankEst{}\%).

\paragraph{Second recogniser and second dataset} With a ResNet-50 recogniser trained on the same data (\RecRfValAcc{}\% held-out accuracy), our model raises the rank-1 rate from \RecEarvnRfRankBic{}\% to \RecEarvnRfRankEst{}\% (\RecEarvnRfEstVsBic{} points), against \RecEarvnRfPubVsBic{} for the published weights (Table~\ref{tab:recogrobust}). On AWEx (\RecAwexEnrolled{} subjects enrolled, \RecAwexProbes{} probes, never used for training), no method changes the rate significantly with either recogniser; the confidence intervals, about 8 points wide, exclude only gains of the EarVN1.0 size. AWEx differs in two respects, which we examined after obtaining this result. Its probes show no JPEG block structure (median block-boundary ratio \BlockAwex{}, against \BlockEarvnLow{} and \BlockEarvnHigh{} for the two EarVN1.0 groups), so a compression-aware model has little to correct. And its headroom, the gap between the rank-1 rates of large and upscaled small probes, is close to zero (\RecAwexReRankRef{}\% against \RecAwexReRankBic{}\%, with a median of \RecAwexGalMed{} enrolled images per subject), whereas on EarVN1.0 it is large (\RecRankRef{}\% against \RecRankBic{}\%); within EarVN1.0 the gain tends to grow with the headroom of a subject, though not significantly (\ref{app:headroom}). Upscaling thus improved identification where probes were compressed and the recogniser lost accuracy at low resolution, but our two datasets cannot separate these conditions, and the results do not show that SR improves ear identification in general. Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative} shows examples for illustration only.

\subsection{Alternatives that account for compression}
\label{sec:alternatives}
Two alternatives remain: removing compression artefacts with a separate network before upscaling, and large published SR models trained with synthetic degradations that include compression. We evaluate both with published weights on the same inputs: FBCNN~\cite{jiang2021fbcnn} (\RevFbcnnParams{}M parameters, blind) followed by bicubic interpolation or the published SPAN, and BSRNet~\cite{zhang2021bsrgan} and Real-ESRNet~\cite{wang2021realesrgan}, the fidelity-oriented RRDB versions of BSRGAN and Real-ESRGAN. We also fine-tune RRDB itself with our procedure, which isolates the effect of architecture under identical training (Table~\ref{tab:review}).

\paragraph{Compression removal before upscaling} FBCNN followed by SPAN improves on interpolation by \RevFbVsBicMin{} to \RevFbVsBicMax{}~dB on compressed inputs. Against our model it is \RevFbVsEstAmiMin{} to \RevFbVsEstAmiMax{}~dB on compressed AMI inputs, \RevFbVsEstWildJpegMin{} to \RevFbVsEstWildJpegMax{}~dB on JPEG-only in-the-wild inputs and \RevFbVsEstWildEstMin{} to \RevFbVsEstWildEstMax{}~dB on measured ones, and it keeps more accuracy on clean inputs. Its rank-1 rate on EarVN1.0 (\RevRankFb{}\% and \RevRankFbRf{}\% with the two recognisers) is above bicubic upscaling but below our model (\RevFbVsEstRank{} and \RevFbVsEstRankRf{} points). It has \RevFbParamsRatio{} times the parameters of SPAN, takes \RevFbLat{} against \RevSpanLat{}~ms on the desktop GPU, and on the smartphone, where FBCNN must run in 32-bit precision (\ref{app:coreml}), needs \LatPhoneFbCpu{}~ms on the CPU and \LatPhoneFbAll{}~ms with all units, against \LatPhoneSpanFCpu{} and \LatPhoneSpanFAll{}~ms for SPAN at the same precision. At 32-bit precision Core ML runs both networks on the GPU rather than the neural engine, and for a network as small as SPAN the GPU path is slower than the CPU, possibly because of scheduling overhead.

\paragraph{Large models trained with synthetic degradations} BSRNet stays within \RevBsrVsBicMin{} to \RevBsrVsBicMax{}~dB of interpolation on compressed inputs and Real-ESRNet falls below it, yet both identify real probes well: BSRNet reaches \RevRankBsr{}\% and \RevRankBsrRf{}\% (\RevBsrVsEstRank{} and \RevBsrVsEstRankRf{} points relative to our model), Real-ESRNet \RevRankResr{}\% and \RevRankResrRf{}\%. With the ResNet-50 recogniser both exceed our model, at \ParamsRatio{} times the parameters and \RevBsrLatRatio{} times the latency.

\paragraph{The same architecture with our training} Fine-tuned with our procedure on the same five folds (mixed precision, to fit memory; all runs pass the stress test), RRDB gains \RevRrdbGainQMin{} to \RevRrdbGainQMax{}~dB over its published weights at JPEG 75, against \RevSpanGainQMin{} to \RevSpanGainQMax{}~dB for SPAN, and exceeds the fine-tuned SPAN by only \RevRrdbVsEstQMin{} to \RevRrdbVsEstQMax{}~dB (\RevRrdbVsEstMin{} to \RevRrdbVsEstMax{}~dB over all compressed inputs). Its rank-1 rate is \RevRankRrdb{}\% and \RevRankRrdbRf{}\% (\RevRrdbVsEstRank{} and \RevRrdbVsEstRankRf{} points relative to SPAN). A network with \ParamsRatio{} times more parameters thus adds a fraction of a decibel, whereas the training degradation changes either network by \RevSpanGainQMin{} to \RevRrdbGainQMax{}~dB.

Every method that accounts for compression improved identification on EarVN1.0, and no bicubic-trained model did. PSNR and identification rank the methods differently: BSRNet has a PSNR close to interpolation and one of the highest rank-1 rates (the highest with the ResNet-50 recogniser), whereas FBCNN followed by SPAN has a higher PSNR and a lower rank-1 rate. With the same SPAN network, fine-tuning with compression identifies better than placing a deblocking network in front of it (\RevRankEst{}\% against \RevRankFb{}\%), without the second network. Among the methods that improved identification, the fine-tuned SPAN has the fewest parameters and the lowest latency.

\paragraph{Fidelity and identification} Across the \CorrNAll{} methods with identification results, the rank-1 rate on real EarVN1.0 probes follows LPIPS measured on synthesised EarVN1.0 inputs (Spearman $\rho=\CorrLpipsAll$ and $\CorrLpipsAllRf$ for the two recognisers), less closely DISTS ($\CorrDistsAll$ and $\CorrDistsAllRf$), and not PSNR ($\CorrPsnrAll$ and $\CorrPsnrAllRf$; Table~\ref{tab:fidrecog}). The two GAN-trained models cause most of the difference: without them PSNR is also related to identification ($\rho=\CorrPsnrNogan$ over \CorrNNogan{} methods), and within the \CorrNLone{} L1-trained models fine-tuned in this study PSNR and LPIPS track it about equally well ($\CorrPsnrLone$ and $\CorrLpipsLone$). The methods are not independent, the fidelity measures are computed on synthesised inputs of partly different subjects and against EarVN1.0 ground truth that is itself compressed (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}), and the analysis was made after the identification results were known, so these correlations describe this dataset rather than establish a general rule. PSNR thus loses its relation with identification once GAN-trained upscalers are compared, whereas LPIPS keeps it in all three groups. LPIPS relies on ImageNet features~\cite{zhang2018lpips} and the recognisers on ear images, so the agreement is not built into the measures.

\subsection{Efficiency}
\label{sec:efficiency}
The proposed training leaves the cost of the backbone unchanged. We define real time as a median latency of at most 33~ms for one $48\times68$ input, the largest size considered. On an RTX~3080, SPAN takes \LatSpan{}~ms and DISP \LatDisp{}~ms, against \LatBsrgan{}~ms for BSRGAN. On an \PhoneName{} (\PhoneChip{}, iOS \PhoneOs{}, released in 2020), with Core ML in 16-bit precision and the Xcode performance tool, SPAN takes \LatPhoneSpanCpu{}~ms on the CPU and \LatPhoneSpanAll{}~ms with all compute units, and DISP \LatPhoneDispCpu{} and \LatPhoneDispAll{}~ms (Table~\ref{tab:latencyios}). The RRDB architecture of BSRGAN, BSRNet, Real-ESRNet and the fine-tuned RRDB takes \LatPhoneRrdbCpu{}~ms on the CPU and \LatPhoneRrdbAll{}~ms on the neural engine, so it meets the threshold only with the accelerator; SwinIR-light misses it in both configurations (\LatPhoneSwinCpu{} and \LatPhoneSwinAll{}~ms), as \PhoneSwinOpsCpu{} of its \PhoneSwinOps{} operations stay on the CPU. On accelerated hardware a large model is therefore also real-time, and the small network's advantage becomes the time and memory left for other steps. Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff} combines latency and identification: below the threshold on the CPU, only the compression-trained small networks improve identification markedly, and every method with a higher rank-1 rate needs about 100~ms.
