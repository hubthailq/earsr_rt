\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the results add} The loss of bicubic-trained SR models on compressed natural images was known (Section~\ref{sec:related}). Our measurements locate it: the excess that every model adds to the compression error grows with compression, lies mainly around the Nyquist frequency of the input, and overtakes the interpolation error saved at a compression level that depends on image content. For ear images this level falls between the two quality levels most common in real small images. The condition of Equation~\ref{eq:sign} also describes WebP and AVIF, whereas a threshold on $E_c/E_i$ applies only within one codec. The fine-tuning results follow from this: for fidelity under compression, the training degradation changes a network by more than a decibel and the architecture by a fraction of a decibel. Identification ranks the methods differently from PSNR, so these fidelity results do not carry over to identification without a separate test.

\paragraph{Identification on AWEx} We did not anticipate the absence of a gain on AWEx, and the following explanation was formulated after the result; we offer it as a hypothesis. AWEx probes show no JPEG block structure (median block-boundary ratio \BlockAwex{}, against \BlockEarvnLow{} and \BlockEarvnHigh{} for the two EarVN1.0 quality groups), and their headroom, the gap between the rank-1 rates of large and upscaled small probes, is close to zero (\RecAwexReRankRef{}\% against \RecAwexReRankBic{}\% with Ear-R18), whereas on EarVN1.0 it is large (\RecRankRef{}\% against \RecRankBic{}\%). Synthesised probes, on which models trained with compression helped only at 24 pixels and JPEG 75 (\ref{app:recogsynth}), are consistent with both conditions, but they are easier than real probes and were evaluated only with recognisers trained on large images, and per-subject headroom does not predict the gain significantly (\ref{app:headroom}).

\paragraph{Practical implications} For compressed ear images, a published efficient SR model may not improve on interpolation, and fine-tuning on ear images does not help while the degradation is left unchanged. Training with JPEG compression over the quality range of the target images was sufficient; the range can be read from the image files. When fine-tuning data come from controlled acquisition, photometric augmentation and a brightness stress test prevent failures on in-the-wild images. Where inputs are not compressed, the published weights are preferable for fidelity. Where cost is not a constraint, large models trained with synthetic degradations identify real probes as well or better, with a PSNR close to interpolation.

\paragraph{Limitations} The error-ratio analysis is empirical, covers bicubic-trained $\times4$ models and three codecs, and locates the sign change only to within the measured quality levels. Fidelity is measured on synthesised inputs, against ground truth that is near-clean on AMI only (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}); processing of real images such as resampling after compression was not tested. The adapted model is below the published weights on clean inputs and does not give the highest identification rate: BSRNet and Real-ESRNet exceed it with one of two recognisers. The identification gain was observed on one dataset (EarVN1.0, \RecSubj{} subjects), with low absolute rates and recognisers trained by us, whose rates are not comparable with benchmarks such as UERC~\cite{emersic2019uerc}. AMI has 100 subjects, training variation was measured on one fold, and the smartphone latencies are medians.

\section{Conclusion}
Efficient SR models trained with bicubic downsampling add an excess to the compression error of their input, in every setting we measured and with three codecs. The excess grows with compression while the interpolation error they save does not, so the gain over interpolation changes sign at a compression level set by image content. Ear images are smooth, and this level lies between the compression levels typical of real small ear images. A published real-time network fine-tuned with JPEG compression over the measured range of quality levels improves on interpolation and on the published models on three datasets at no extra cost, and on EarVN1.0 it raised the rank-1 rate of real small probes with recognisers trained on upscaled images, but not on AWEx. The benchmark, the measured degradation and the trained models will be released to support work on architectures designed for such inputs.
